# UXCommunity community guidelines

These guidelines apply to every member and to every surface on UXCommunity:
profiles, direct messages, community chat, threads, showcase posts, resources,
events, community descriptions and job listings. They apply equally on the web
app and the mobile app.

UXCommunity is a professional design community. The short version: be decent,
be honest, and do not use the platform to harm or defraud anyone.

## 1. Harassment and hate

Do not:

- target a person with insults, slurs, sexualised comments, or repeated
  unwanted contact;
- organise others to harass someone, including through dogpiling, mass
  reporting, or brigading a thread or community;
- attack people for their race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability,
  age, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, or other protected
  characteristic;
- publish someone's private information (see section 8);
- evade a block or a moderation action to continue contacting someone.

Criticism of work is allowed. Critique the work, not the person. Sustained
personal hostility is not critique.

## 2. Threats and violence

Do not threaten violence, incite violence, celebrate or encourage harm, or
share instructions for causing harm. This includes "jokes" that a reasonable
person would read as a threat. Credible threats may be reported to the
authorities and the account may be removed immediately.

## 3. Spam and scams

Do not:

- post repetitive or unrelated promotional content;
- run fake giveaways, phishing links, or credential-collection pages;
- advertise "guaranteed" jobs, clients, or income that do not exist;
- pressure members into paying for an opportunity, a referral, a portfolio
  review, or an introduction;
- use automated accounts, bulk messaging, or engagement farming.

Self-promotion is allowed where the community's topic makes it relevant. A
portfolio link in your profile is fine; a sales pitch in every thread is not.

## 4. Fraudulent job listings and recruitment

Job listings must describe a real role for a real company. Do not:

- post a role that does not exist, or that has already been filled, without
  saying so;
- impersonate a company or a recruiter;
- ask an applicant for payment, a "training fee", a deposit, or bank details;
- request sensitive documents (government ID, bank statements, payslips) as a
  condition of applying;
- use a listing to harvest portfolios, contact details, or résumés for
  unrelated purposes;
- include requirements that discriminate on a protected characteristic
  (section 7).

Applications and applicant data are private between the applicant and the
poster. A listing that is removed for moderation is deactivated; its
applications are not deleted by the moderation action.

## 5. Impersonation

Do not pretend to be another person, a company you do not represent, a
UXCommunity moderator or administrator, or a verification reviewer. Parody and
fan accounts must say clearly that they are not the person or brand they refer
to.

## 6. Sexual and inappropriate content

Do not post:

- pornography, sexual imagery, or sexual content involving minors (which is
  reported to the authorities);
- graphic violence, gore, or content intended to shock;
- content that sexualises a real person without consent;
- harassment-adjacent "exposure" content, including non-consensual intimate
  imagery.

Design work that includes nudity in an artistic or editorial context is
permitted where it is clearly labelled and not sexualised. When in doubt, ask a
moderator before posting.

## 7. Discrimination

Do not discriminate against, exclude, or demean people on the basis of a
protected characteristic, including in:

- job requirements or "culture fit" language;
- community rules or membership decisions;
- comments, critiques, or hiring feedback.

Reasonable accessibility accommodations are not discrimination.

## 8. Personal information

Do not publish another person's private information without their consent,
including their home address, phone number, personal email, workplace, family
details, financial information, or private messages. Sharing your own contact
details is your choice; sharing someone else's is not.

## 9. Illegal activity

Do not use UXCommunity to plan, organise, advertise, or facilitate illegal
activity, including fraud, trafficking, the sale of illegal goods, or the
distribution of malware. Content that puts a person at imminent risk may be
preserved and reported to the relevant authorities.

## 10. Repeat offenders and account restrictions

Moderation is cumulative. A first violation usually results in a warning or
content removal. Repeated violations, or a single severe violation, can lead
to:

- a temporary suspension (up to 30 days);
- a permanent ban;
- removal of a community or deactivation of a job listing.

A banned member may appeal once. Evading a ban with a new account is itself a
violation and may result in the new account being banned as well.

## Reporting

Every report is reviewed by the UXCommunity team. You can report:

- a profile;
- a comment or reply;
- a community chat message;
- an entire community;
- a job listing.

Reports are confidential: the reported member is never told who reported them.
Reporting is not a way to win an argument, and knowingly false reports can be
moderated.

## Appeals

If content was removed or an account was restricted, the affected member can
appeal the decision once from **Moderation appeals** in their dashboard. An
appeal should explain what happened and why the decision should be reviewed.

## Enforcement expectations

Moderation is best-effort, not a 24/7 staffed service. The team aims to review:

| Severity | Examples | Target first response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | credible threat, sexual content involving minors, imminent danger | within 24 hours |
| High | harassment campaign, scam, fraudulent job, doxxing | within 48 hours |
| Standard | spam, misleading listing, inappropriate content | within 5 business days |
| Low | off-topic, minor profile issue | within 10 business days |

These are targets, not guarantees. Complex cases, appeals, and investigations
take longer. See [moderation-policy.md](moderation-policy.md) for how decisions
are made and [report-handling-procedure.md](report-handling-procedure.md) for
the operational process.
